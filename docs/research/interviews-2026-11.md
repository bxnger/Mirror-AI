# Problem interviews — November 2026

7 respondents (school and college students), interviewed by text message with the same five questions.
Answers are anonymized (R1–R7) and paraphrased; quotes are kept verbatim where marked. Raw chat
screenshots are not stored in the repository because they contain respondents' nicknames and photos.

## Questions

1. What are your goals besides studying?
2. How did you plan your preparation last time?
3. Has a plan of yours ever failed?
4. How do you know an approach works? Did you change your strategy?
5. What would you change in how you plan?

## Answers

| # | Goals | How they plan | Plan failed? | How they judge progress | Would change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | personal goal | — | yes: "didn't have time" | "it's noticeable" | "idk" |
| R2 | finish internship, earn money for purchases | "thoroughly": decides if it is needed and why | no: "never happened" | notices gradual progress | nothing: "everything suits me" |

## Summary

- **Plans fail for 6 of 7.** Reasons: no time, tiredness after school, procrastination, a plan that was too strict, other priorities.
- **6 of 7 judge progress by results** (mock tests, before/after comparison, finished tasks) — they already do a manual "reality check".
- **4 of 7 name procrastination or distraction** as what they would change.
- **3 of 7 would change nothing** (R1, R2, R4) — a segment that does not feel the pain; harder to convert.
- Key quote (R3): «Слишком жёстко всё расписал, не учёл, что после школы вообще ничего не хочется делать».

## What it means for MIRROR AI

- Supports the hypothesis: plans break because they are disconnected from real capacity, not because there is no plan.
  → Reality Check + adaptation to the observed pace (shorter sessions, realistic hours) is the core value.
- Respondents already measure by results → the "Measure" step matches an existing habit.
- Small qualitative sample (n = 7), partly outside the core segment (R4 is not studying, R3 takes the Russian OGE).
